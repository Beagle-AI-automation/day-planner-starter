Beagle 7-day trial · Day 6 · explainer

From your laptop to a live website

What you are looking at. A bench with two levels. The upper shelf is GitHub, where repos live online; the lower bench is your laptop. A repo (one project's folder of files) is drawn as a crate, and every step makes a copy rather than moving the only one. The five buttons on the front panel are the five steps of Day 6 in the order they happen: Fork copies the template crate into your bay on the shelf, Clone sends a copy down to your laptop while the bay keeps its crate, Commit seals the laptop copy with a labelled message, Push sends the sealed copy back up so GitHub's crate matches yours, and Pages slides it into the kiosk, which unpacks it and shows index.html to anyone who has the link. The readout at the bottom right tells you where the crate is and, at the end, the shape of your new web address.

Try it. Press the five numbered buttons in order, using the row under the figure (the small numbered caps on the panel do the same). Press one too early (Push before Commit, say) and the red lamp lights and tells you why. Nothing moves until you press; Watch a demo runs the five steps once, with one wrong press on the way, then hands control back to you. Keyboard: Tab to a button and press Enter, or focus the figure and use keys 1 to 5, Enter for the next step, R to reset, D for the demo.

Words used

  • Repo: short for repository: one project's folder of files, kept with its full history. On this bench a repo is a crate, and every copy of it is another crate.
  • Template repo: a repo GitHub lets you copy as a starting point. The fixed crate on the far left of the upper shelf.
  • Fork: make your own copy of someone else's repo on GitHub. The copy lands in your bay on the upper shelf; the template stays where it was.
  • Clone: download a copy of a GitHub repo to your laptop. The left lift lowers a copy to the bench, and your fork stays in its bay on the shelf.
  • Commit: save a labelled snapshot of your changes, with a short message. A snapshot is a saved version you can always go back to. The press seals the crate and sticks the label on.
  • Push: send your sealed commits from the laptop up to GitHub, so GitHub's copy matches yours. The right lift carries the sealed crate up, and the crate in your bay takes the same seal.
  • GitHub Pages: a free GitHub feature that serves a repo's files as a public website: a computer at GitHub hands index.html to any browser that asks for the link. The kiosk unpacks the crate and shows the page.
  • URL: a web address. Pages gives you one shaped like username.github.io/repo: your GitHub username, then the repo name.
