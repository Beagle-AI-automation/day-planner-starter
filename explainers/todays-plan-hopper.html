How planToday decides what you do today

What you are looking at. A sorting machine. Each block is one task from the sample data: its height is the hours the task needs, its colour is the type, and the grooves on top are the type's weight. The blocks queue on a tilted chute in the order prioritise() gives them. At the gate halfway down, the machine looks at the hours still available (the frame in the hopper below) and decides: a block that fits drops through the door into the hopper, which is today's plan (chosen); a block that does not fit slides past to the tray at the bottom (deferred, left for another day). A block with a red tag is overdue, so the door opens for it no matter what.

Try it. Turn the dial (or drag the slider) to change the hours you have today, and step the date to make more tasks overdue. The machine re-sorts the twelve blocks every time, and the table under the figure shows each decision as it happens. With the keyboard: click the figure once, then use the arrow keys and space.

The rule in plain English

  1. Sort first. prioritise(tasks, today) puts overdue tasks (due date already past) at the front, then the rest by due date, soonest first. Two tasks due the same day: the heavier type goes first (exam 4, practical 3, record 2, assignment 1, from taskWeight), then A to Z by title.
  2. Then walk the list once. planToday starts with hoursLeft = hoursAvailable and looks at each task in that order.
  3. Fits? Chosen. If the task's hours are not more than hoursLeft, it goes into chosen and its hours are taken off hoursLeft. A task that uses up exactly what is left still fits.
  4. Overdue? Chosen anyway, even if it does not fit; hoursLeft can go negative, which is why nothing after it fits.
  5. Otherwise, deferred. The task is skipped for today, but the walk continues: a smaller task further down can still fit in the hours left.

Sample data only: six made-up courses and twelve made-up tasks. The explainer uses whole calendar days to decide "overdue", and today is the date you set, not your laptop's clock.
