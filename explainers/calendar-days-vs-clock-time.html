Why a task due tomorrow says “Due today”

You are looking at a small bench. On the wall hang a 24-hour clock (its short hand goes round once a day, with midnight at the top and noon at the bottom; the long hand goes round once an hour, as on any clock) and a month calendar with two pins: the circled square is today, the other pin is the due date. On the bench, a dial gauge measures the gap from now to the due date in days, as a decimal such as 0.42. Beside it, a stepped chute rounds that decimal down to the whole number beneath it: programmers call this rounding-down “floor”. A follower foot rests on the step for −1, 0, 1 or 2 days. Under the pins, a short ruler counts calendar squares instead, so it can only ever read whole days. The program with the bug uses the gauge and the chute; the fixed program uses the ruler.

Try it: keep Tomorrow selected and drag the time slider from 00:00 to 23:59. The moment the clock passes midnight the gauge falls below 1.00 (0.999 days at 00:01, 0.50 at 12:00, 0.001 at 23:59), the foot drops to step 0, and the badge says “Due today”. The ruler stays at 1 square the whole day.

A short demo is running. The clock walks itself through 00:00, 00:01, 12:00 and 23:59 so you can watch the badge flip. Touch any control, or drag across the bench, to take over.

Program with the bug · clock arithmetic

Fixed program · calendar squares

Due date.
Most apps store a due date as a moment: the first second of that day, 00:00. “Due tomorrow” is tomorrow at 00:00, not tomorrow at bedtime.
Decimal days.
Subtracting two moments gives a length of time. 10 hours is 10 ÷ 24 = 0.42 of a day.
Floor.
Rounding down to the whole number beneath: floor(0.42) = 0, floor(0.99) = 0, floor(−0.4) = −1. The chute does this with gravity.
Off-by-one bug.
A mistake in a program where an answer is out by exactly one. Here, one whole day: “Due today” instead of “Due tomorrow”.
Synthetic data.
The month, the pins and the times are made up for this figure.
